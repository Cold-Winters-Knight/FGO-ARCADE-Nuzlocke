# FGO Arcade Nuzlocke Rules

A solo challenge format for **Fate/Grand Order Arcade** built around limited summoning, permadeath, roster investment, and narrative play.

The goal is not to perfectly recreate retail FGO Arcade or produce a perfectly balanced competitive ruleset. The goal is to let you experience **your own Grand Order**: the gacha determines who answers your summons, deaths change the shape of the campaign, and limited resources force decisions about which Servants become the core of your run.

## Summoning / Pulls

At the **start of each Singularity**, gain **50 pulls**.

Those pulls may be spent on:

- the current Singularity banner;
- any previously unlocked Singularity banner;
- any combination of those banners.

**Saving pulls is allowed.** Unspent pulls carry forward.

The custom Singularity banners are curated around:

- story relevance;
- era relevance;
- Servant rarity.

The intent is for each chapter to have a recognizable locality to the story progression instead of every new Servant permanently diluting one enormous pool.

The current custom banners use approximately **18% Servants / 82% Craft Essences**, averaging about **9 Servants per 50 pulls**.

Seasonal, event-themed, gag, and other eligible alternate forms are generally kept out of the normal story pools so they can be handled through **Digivolution**.

## Starter Servant

Every player begins the run with **one Servant of their choice at NP1**.

There are no restrictions. Pick a favorite, something thematic, or something absurdly strong if that is the run you want.

Not all Servants are created equal, so starter choice is partially a self-selected difficulty setting.

## The Mystery Box

At the **start of every Singularity**, gain:

**1 (ONE) Mystery Box summon.**

The Mystery Box is:

**100% Servants. No CEs.**

Its pool consists of standalone Servants that do not have a sensible home in the normal story/era banners.

### Mystery Box summons are duplicate-proof

If you roll a Servant you have already received from the Mystery Box, reroll until you receive somebody new.

A dead Mystery Servant still counts as previously obtained. Death does not turn the Mystery Box into a resurrection machine.

Mystery Box Servants cannot gain NP levels through additional Mystery Box summons.

That is what **NP Tokens** are for.

## NP Tokens

Completing a Singularity awards:

**1 (ONE) NP Token.**

An NP Token may normally be spent on **any currently owned Servant** to increase their NP level by 1, up to NP5.

For normal Servants, this supplements ordinary duplicate progression.

For Mystery Box Servants:

**NP Tokens are the ONLY way to increase their NP level OR gain an eligible variant.**

NP Tokens normally function only as NP-level increases and do not count as duplicate summons. **Mystery Box Servants are the exception:** once one reaches NP3, a Token may instead be spent on the same NP4/NP5-or-Digivolution choice normally created by a duplicate.

This creates an opportunity cost. Do you strengthen the strange Mystery Servant you fell in love with, push your starter toward NP3, repair bad duplicate luck, or save the token for later?

Outside of the Mystery Box exception above, NP Tokens do not count as duplicate summons and cannot trigger Digivolution.

## Permadeath

Track your deaths.

If a Servant dies during a mission:

**They're gone.**

That Servant is permanently exiled from the run unless a Command Spell specifically saves or resurrects them.

If the gacha later summons that dead Servant again:

**It's a dead draw.**

The pull is consumed. The Servant does not return.

This is intentionally harsh. Losing a developed NP3+ Servant should change the shape of the run rather than merely costing upgrade materials.

## Support Servants / Fail State

### Soft-lock concerns

From the beginning of the run, your roster is guaranteed to contain at least three owned Servants:

- **Mash**
- **your chosen Starter Servant**
- **your first Mystery Box Servant**

Because of that, even pulling **0 Servants in all 50 normal pulls** cannot soft-lock you out of starting with a three-person team.

### Support Servants are illegal. If you cant field a team of 3 owned servants, that's a loss state.
(Still trying to think of work arounds for this, I want servants to be able to solo missions but im not familiar enough with Arcade yet to make a good rule about it.)

**Support Servants may not be used.**

The game's support system exists to fill roster gaps, but those Servants are not part of your Nuzlocke roster and do not count as owned Servants for the purposes of the run.

### Run failure

If you **end a mission with no surviving Servants that you own**, the run is over.

This is the campaign fail state.

## Command Spells

Players get **3 (THREE) Command Spells for the entire run**.

They do not naturally replenish.

A Command Spell lets you **break one rule, one time**.

There are three formally recognized uses.

### Resurrection

Prevent the death of a Servant or return one previously dead Servant to the run.

A resurrected Servant returns with the progression they had when they died.

This is the conventional way to undo permanent exile.

### Clairvoyance

Immediately rewind an absolutely catastrophic mission.

All deaths and consequences from that failed attempt are ignored, and you may immediately retry the mission.

You may:

- change your Servants;
- change your CEs;
- rethink your strategy.

You may **not**:

- farm;
- summon;
- progress elsewhere;
- stockpile resources before retrying.

You saw the doomed timeline and rejected it.

### Unleash Your NP

Manifest **1 additional NP Token** and immediately spend it on one of your currently owned Servants.

The bonus token cannot be banked for later.

Burn the Seal. Power somebody up. Deal with the consequences.

**The NP Token created by Unleash Your NP cannot be used to trigger Digivolution, even for a Mystery Box Servant.**

### Creative Command Spell uses are encouraged

The three uses above are examples, **not an exhaustive spell list**.

This is Fate.

If you come up with a dramatic, narratively appropriate use of a Command Spell that breaks one rule, use it. Narrative play is heavily encouraged.

Command Spells are supposed to be ridiculous emergency miracles.

There are only two hard restrictions.

#### Command Spells cannot manipulate the gacha

No:

- target summoning;
- changing a pull result;
- rerolling a normal banner result;
- creating extra normal summons;
- summoning a CE or Servant you never obtained.

Command Spells affect Servants already bound to your Master.

#### Command Spells cannot trigger Digivolution

Alternate forms have their own progression rule.

No cheating evolution with a Seal.

## Digivolution

Some Arcade Servants have multiple playable forms: alternate classes, seasonal forms, Alters, and similar variants.

Rather than cluttering the normal gacha with multiple versions of the same character, eligible variants are removed from the normal summon pools and become progression rewards.

Once a Servant reaches **NP3**, every future duplicate presents a choice:

**Go tall or go wide.**

You may either:

**Increase the original Servant's NP level by 1, up to NP5**

**OR**

**Unlock one eligible alternate version of that Servant at NP1.**

For example, an NP3 Servant could eventually become:

> NP5 base form

or

> NP3 base + two alternate forms

or

> NP4 base + one alternate form

If the original is already NP5, additional duplicates may be spent unlocking remaining eligible variants.

Each playable variant counts as its **own Servant for permadeath purposes**. Losing one form does not automatically kill the rest of the family tree.

**A variant lost to permadeath cannot simply be Digivolved again.** That form is dead for the remainder of the run unless restored through a Command Spell.

Why do I need to independently pull Summer Scáthach?

**She's right there. She is perfectly capable of putting on a swimsuit.**

## Current local-server inventory workaround

FGO Arcade was originally built around the assumption that summoned cards would be **physically printed and scanned back into the cabinet**, rather than maintaining a normal always-available digital card inventory while the game is already running.

That creates a disconnect in the current local-server setup.

**Current workaround:** after summoning new cards, relaunch the game with the cards your account now owns so the updated roster is available for the next mission.

This is an implementation limitation of the current local setup, not an additional challenge rule, and can be removed if the launcher/server workflow later gains a cleaner way to refresh owned cards without restarting the game.

## The point of the rules

The rules are meant to generate stories and present difficult decisions.

> **Gacha luck determines what tools you receive.**  
> **Permadeath determines what tools you keep.**  
> **Duplicates make you choose power vs. roster depth.**  
> **NP Tokens make you choose where to invest limited progression.**  
> **Mystery Boxes inject strange Servants your normal route might never see.**  
> **Three Command Spells let you occasionally utilize Protagonist: EX.**

The intended answer should not always be "reroll until you have the meta unit" or "farm until you're stronger."

The randomness is part of the story.

Sometimes you get an all-star. Sometimes some Servant you barely cared about becomes the only survivor who has been with you for four Singularities, and suddenly you would burn one of your three reality-warping Command Spells before letting them die.
